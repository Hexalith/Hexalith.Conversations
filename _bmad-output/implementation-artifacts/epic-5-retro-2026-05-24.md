## Challenges And Friction

Amelia (Developer): "The dominant friction across the verification suites was the content-safety blocklist colliding with the natural vocabulary of the domain being verified. The `UnsafeTerms` list is a case-insensitive substring scan over 31 terms, and the very words these suites describe — `tenant-`, `provider-session`, `stream`, `store`, `envelope`, `exception`, `redacted content`, `sequence` — are on it. Almost every suite story burned a debug cycle renaming a SuiteId, a scenario token, a carry-forward reference, or a SafeMessage to get past `EnsureContentSafe`."

Winston (System Architect): "There was a recurring tension between the seven-value closed `ReleaseGateId` vocabulary and the mapping tokens used inside suite results. Idempotency (5.6) and platform-evidence (5.11) are legitimate verification concerns but are not among the seven release gates, so their manifest `releaseGateId` had to be `null` even though `ReleaseGateMappings` could carry `"idempotency"` / `"platform-evidence"`. Two stories had to discover or correct this distinction, including Story 5.6 fixing its own Dev Notes."

Murat (Test Architect): "Some of the proof is structural rather than executed against production behavior. The verification suites validate that fixtures describing the correct outcomes serialize, classify, and stay content-safe — they are the second-level release-gating aggregation, deliberately carrying forward the first-level production proof from Epics 1-4 rather than re-running it. That is the correct design per the Two-Level Evidence gate, but it means a reader must follow `carryForwardCommitmentRef` to the owning story to see the executed production behavior. The release artifact and manifest fixtures are also synthetic; real GA signing happens at release time using this tooling."

Recurring challenges:

- Content-safety blocklist collisions were the single most common debug-log entry across 5.4-5.11. The story Dev Notes repeatedly under-specified the blocklist (5.9 missed "stream"; 5.10 missed "envelope"), and the runtime validator was the authoritative check.
- The CS8122 expression-tree pitfall (`is null` vs `== null` inside `ShouldAllBe` lambdas) recurred enough that it became boilerplate carry-forward guidance in every suite story from 5.5 onward.
- `ConformanceCheck` has no dedicated redaction, portability, schema-evolution, or platform-evidence check, so each suite reused the closest existing check (`GovernancePrecondition`, `EventPublication`, `CompatibilityDiscovery`). This is defensible but means the check ID alone does not name the concern; the requirement and gate mappings carry that meaning.
- A code-review API hang on Story 5.9 (round 1 sat ~4 minutes with frozen token counts) required killing and respawning the review session — a build-process reliability issue, not a code issue.
- The agent mix shifted mid-build: GPT-5 Codex authored 5.1, then Claude Sonnet 4.6 authored 5.2-5.11 because the codex worker was unreliable on this host (mis-reported state, early-exited on complex create/dev).

## Key Lessons

1. Deferred release evidence is real only when it is built and traceable, not when it is named.

   Epic 1 deferred signing, manifest traceability, the verification suites, portability, and schema-evolution proof. Epic 5 produced concrete artifacts for each (`ReleaseConformanceArtifactV1`, `ConformanceManifestV1` + validator, six suites, FR90 and FR91 suites) with `carryForwardCommitmentRef` links back to the owning foundation story. The handoff worked because the foundation epic named the items precisely and Epic 5 consumed them rather than re-deriving them.

2. A shared closed vocabulary is an asset, but its boundary must be discoverable.

   Reusing `ReleaseGateId`, `ReleaseGateStatus`, `ConformanceCheck`, and `ConformanceContractValidation` kept eleven stories coherent. But the seven-gate closed vocabulary versus the open mapping-token surface caused two stories to make (and one to fix) `releaseGateId` mistakes. When a concern is verified but is not a release gate, the manifest gate field must be `null` and the mapping token carries the concept.

3. Content-safety enforcement must be modeled before authoring, not discovered at runtime.

   The `EnsureContentSafe` substring blocklist is unforgiving and counter-intuitive for security/governance vocabulary. Story Dev Notes that listed only a partial blocklist (missing "stream", "envelope", "store") cost repeated debug cycles. The lesson: treat the full 31-term blocklist as the authoritative input and validate tokens and free text against it before writing, exactly as Story 5.11 did (clean run, no blocklist surprises).

4. Two-level evidence keeps release proof honest without duplicating work.

   Every verification suite (5.5-5.11) is deliberately a release-gating aggregation that carries forward production proof from Epics 1-4 rather than re-implementing it. This is the right separation, but it places a navigation obligation on the reader and on the manifest: the carry-forward reference must point at a real owning story, and the suite must not claim to be the production behavior.

5. Module-versus-platform honesty is a first-class release-evidence requirement.

   Story 5.11 made the buyer-facing distinction between Conversations-owned controls and inherited EventStore/Tenants/Parties/FrontComposer/Dapr/Aspire controls explicit, including marking missing inherited evidence as `unknown-accepted` and incompatible inherited evidence as `blocked`. Not overstating module-level proof for controls that belong elsewhere is itself a release gate concern, not a documentation footnote.

## Team Agreements

- Keep one shared conformance/release vocabulary; extending `ConformanceCheck`, `ConformanceOutcome`, `ReleaseGateId`, or `ReleaseGateStatus` is an ADR-triggering public-contract change.
- Treat the full content-safety blocklist as the authoritative input when authoring tokens, scenario names, carry-forward references, and SafeMessages; validate before writing, not after a runtime panic.
- Honor the closed-vocabulary-versus-mapping-token distinction: manifest `releaseGateId` must be `null` for concerns that are not one of the seven release gates; the mapping token carries the concept.
