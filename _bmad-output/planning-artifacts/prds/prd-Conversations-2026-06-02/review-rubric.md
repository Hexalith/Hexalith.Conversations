# PRD Quality Review — Conversations Boilerplate Reduction

*Validation run 2026-08-18 against the finalized document (status: final, updated 2026-07-14). Read in full: prd.md (732 lines), addendum.md, .memlog.md, .decision-log.md. Cross-references and claimed platform APIs spot-verified on disk.*

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, decision-ready refactor PRD across all seven dimensions — its decision architecture (§2 readiness snapshot, §12 register with dated OQ resolutions and named owners) and its preservation-gate rigor (FR-20's frozen versioned manifest, SM-C1/SM-C2 counter-metrics) are exemplary, and the unusual embedding of the 181-requirement legacy baseline in §14 is deliberate, firewalled, and honestly dispositioned. The residual risks are life-cycle rather than structural: point-in-time "target met" claims baked into a final document that will silently go stale, and an untraced linkage between §14's preserved requirements and FR-20's frozen test denominator — a preserved obligation with no pre-refactor test is guarded on paper only, and the PRD never requires the coverage map that would reveal which ones those are.

## Decision-readiness — strong

The best part of this PRD. §2 "Implementation Decision and Readiness Snapshot" states the approved state as decisions, not considerations: pilot scope enumerated by FR ID, FR-16 explicitly deferred, "FR-20 and SM-C1 are authoritative," the 5% P95 performance gate, and OQ-1 "delegated to architecture and does not block PRD completion." §12 closes four of five open questions with dates (2026-07-14), rationale, and pointers to versioned evidence (`docs/release-evidence/oq-2-target-interpretation-decision-v1.json`); the one that stays open (OQ-1) has a named owner and a concrete gate ("before the corresponding implementation story starts"). Trade-offs are named with what was given up: FR-16's deferral gets its own FR with consequences rather than vanishing; OQ-3 keeps governance/temporal/hydration in Conversations with an explicit reopen path. SM-2 attainment is honestly marked "**provisional** because they come from an accepted low-confidence estimate" — a PRD willing to say its own headline metric is not yet proven. The §14.4/§14.8 legacy release-governance items (Option A/B, GA+90, adopter milestones) are uniformly dispositioned "Open / historical, not current approval," which is exactly the honesty the rubric asks for.

### Findings
- **medium** Point-in-time attainment claims embedded in a final requirements document (§7 SM-1, SM-2) — SM-1 states "Current evidence: 70.43%; target met" and SM-2 carries "(50.00% files / 67.95% LOC)" inside the metric definitions of a `status: final` document. These snapshots date from 2026-07-14; planning work has continued since (sprint-change proposals dated 2026-08-18 exist beside this workspace), and a submodule checkout alone can invalidate measured evidence with no diff to this file. A reader can take the PRD sentence as the verdict when the versioned release-evidence artifacts are the actual authority. *Fix:* rephrase attainment sentences as dated pointers ("as of 2026-07-14 the versioned artifact X records …; the artifact, not this sentence, is authoritative") or strip current-evidence claims from the metric definitions entirely.

## Substance over theater — strong

No furniture found. The Vision (§1) is unmistakably this product's: "roughly 35,800 lines of source, and about half of it is plumbing," "the same 80-line tenant-access handler," "every new business-domain module pays this tax again." It could not swap into another PRD. The four JTBD roles in §3.1 each drive real requirements (release owner → FR-20; technical-module maintainer → FR-10..15), and the three UJ protagonists each name the FRs they realize. §8's cross-cutting NFRs carry product-specific content — "must not introduce synchronous cross-service calls on hot paths," "tolerant of duplicate/out-of-order delivery (Dapr at-least-once)," "metric names, dimensions, and health endpoints are preserved" — not scalable/secure/reliable boilerplate. The nine actors in §14.3 could read as persona theater, but they are load-bearing: each row is a preserved acceptance journey constraining what FR-20 may not delete as plumbing, and §14.2 says so explicitly. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated and bet on: remove the plumbing tax once, in platform-owned homes, using Conversations as the forcing-function pilot, with "both sides matter equally" (§1) carried through to equally weighted SM-1/SM-2 (§7, "per owner decision"). Feature ordering follows the thesis and the risk profile — inventory first (FR-1/2 as "the spine every downstream story traces to"), consume before promote (§5.3 Phase 1 "Low risk, Conversations-internal, conformance-gated"), adopt-and-prove last. Success metrics validate the thesis rather than activity, and both counter-metrics exist with teeth (SM-C1 "inviolable"; SM-C2 numeric). R1's mitigation ("promote only patterns with ≥2 real consumers or a confirmed Conversations need") is the thesis applied to its own main failure mode.

### Findings
- **medium** SM-3's name and target overstate what the pilot can achieve (§7 SM-3 vs §5.2) — "Duplication eliminated … Target: every in-scope promoted pattern has exactly one source of truth" cannot be literally true in-pilot: fleet migration is a named non-goal, so Folders' and Projects' copies of e.g. the tenant-access handler (addendum §E ranks 2–3) persist after pilot acceptance. What the pilot actually delivers is "a single shared home exists and Conversations consumes it"; the N sibling copies remain until the follow-on. As written, SM-3 either fails at pilot close or gets quietly reinterpreted. *Fix:* redefine SM-3 as "each in-scope promoted pattern has a single shared home with its own tests, and Conversations consumes it; sibling-module copies are retired in the fleet-migration follow-on."

## Done-ness clarity — strong

For the twenty refactor FRs this is exemplary. Every FR carries "Consequences (testable)" that are genuinely verifiable: FR-13 "No Conversations-local AppHost, Aspire, ServiceDefaults, or equivalent runtime-host module remains"; FR-8 "byte/shape-compatible (round-trip tests green)"; FR-15 "Emitted metric names and cardinality are preserved"; FR-17 "deleted (not merely bypassed)." FR-19 and FR-20 specify evidence artifacts down to "frozen file/LOC inclusion rules, source paths, measurement command/tool versions, commit/build identity, results, and named acceptance." The FR-3/FR-10/FR-13 acceptance-boundary crosswalk (§6.3) pre-empts the most likely duplicate-story failure. Judgment-scoped phrases exist (FR-7 "where the platform already provides equivalent behavior") but each is backstopped by a concrete gate ("pure aggregate tests green"). §14's Feature-FRs are capability statements, but §14.1 is explicit that they are a preserved baseline, not pilot work, so their softer form is appropriate.

### Findings
- **medium** The §14 ↔ FR-20 enforcement linkage is only as strong as pre-refactor test coverage, and nothing requires mapping it (§0, §14.1, FR-20) — §0 says §14 "constrains the refactor," and §14.2 closes with "These obligations define what the refactor may not delete or weaken as 'plumbing.'" But FR-20's frozen denominator is "the exact set of passing release-gate conformance tests" from the pre-refactor build: any of the 181 Feature-FR/NFRs with no passing pre-refactor test is preserved on paper only, invisible to the gate. Feature-FR83/84 (manifest-to-requirement traceability) are themselves preserved obligations, not pilot acceptance. A story author deciding whether some code is deletable plumbing cannot tell from this PRD whether a §14 obligation has a manifested test guarding it. FR-2's dispute mechanism partially compensates, but only if someone notices. *Fix:* add a consequence to FR-20 (or to the FR-1 inventory artifact) requiring the preservation manifest to record which Feature-FR/Feature-NFR each denominator test traces to and to list §14 obligations with no test coverage as explicitly unguarded.
- **low** FR-18's template validation names no mechanism (§6.4 FR-18) — "The template is validated against the post-refactor Conversations module (it describes what Conversations actually does)" specifies no check, reviewer, or evidence artifact, in sharp contrast to FR-19's exacting artifact spec one requirement later. Template-vs-module drift after the pilot has no detector. *Fix:* name the validation act (e.g., a recorded walkthrough mapping each template step to the post-refactor module, attached to pilot acceptance).

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and owned. §5.2 gives each non-goal an owner and revisit trigger ("**Owner:** product/platform owner. **Revisit:** after the Conversations pilot passes FR-20"). De-scoping is done in the open — FR-16 is deferred *as a numbered FR with testable consequences*, the cleanest deferral pattern in the document. All eight inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags roundtrip to the §13 index with owners and revisit conditions (verified; the two §9 tags share one row). The eleven open legacy dispositions in §14.8 look like high open-item density, but every one is firewalled from refactor scope ("Legacy defaults are not approvals") and the refactor's own open-item count is effectively one (OQ-1), delegated with a per-story gate — appropriate density for a green-lit initiative. There are no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts, but the Decision and Dependency Register plus the disposition tables do that work more durably for a finalized document. No findings.

This is a chain-top PRD (epics.md already consumes it), and it mostly holds up under extraction. Glossary (§4) defines the load-bearing nouns — Consume/Promote/Keep, authoring surface, release-gate behavior, CORE, plumbing-only test — and they are used consistently. FR-1..FR-20 are contiguous with FR-16 present-as-deferred rather than a gap; Feature-FR1..104 and Feature-NFR1..77 are contiguous with no duplicates. The two namespaces are collision-proof by construction (`FR-3` vs `Feature-FR3` — no hyphen in the legacy form), which is a genuine kindness to downstream tooling. Addendum anchor links from the §6.2/§6.3 tables resolve (verified slugs for §D/§E/§F), and the §14.1 archive link resolves to `_bmad-output/archive/conversations-product-contract-2026-05-31.md` (verified on disk).

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product exactly as the rubric prescribes for an internal developer-platform effort: capability-spec core, UJs deliberately in "lighter form per scope dial" (§3.3) with named protagonists, operational rather than user-facing success metrics, and no UX inflation. Brownfield accuracy is real, not asserted: the claimed platform APIs spot-checked on disk all exist (`EventStoreAggregate<TState>`, `AddEventStoreDomainService`, `IQueryCursorCodec`, `QueryCursorScope`, `TypeMapper`/`NameTypeMapper`), and new refactor journeys are cleanly distinguished from preserved legacy journeys (§3.3 vs §14.3). The boldest shape choice — embedding a 181-requirement product baseline as ~45% of a refactor PRD — is defensible and well-executed: it replaced a live dependency on a superseded root document, and §14.1's disposition paragraph ("'Preserved' does not mean implemented, shipped, accepted, or scheduled") plus the namespace split keep it from leaking into pilot scope. The cost is a dual-purpose document whose title undersells §14's normative authority; acceptable, but worth re-extracting §14 into a standalone product-contract document if a feature initiative reactivates.

### Findings
- **low** Vision retains the superseded plumbing share (§1 vs addendum §A) — §1 asserts "about half of it is plumbing," but the accepted, FR-2-governed baseline reclassified ≈4.7k LOC to Keep, landing at 13,289 LOC (37.15%); the addendum explicitly demotes the ~50% figure to "provenance, not … the accepted baseline." A PRD-only reader carries a number the accepted inventory rejects. *Fix:* qualify §1 ("initially estimated at about half; the accepted baseline is 37.15% — addendum §A") or soften to "a large share."
